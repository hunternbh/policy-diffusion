Global Reporting Regulation, Stock Liquidity, and Policy Diffusion

Abstract
We examine stock liquidity around 17 families of reporting regulation and their international diffusion. The analysis separates national implementation, issuer coverage and actual public reporting. The market panel contains 1,055,142 security-years in 90 jurisdictions. Source-supported comparisons cover eight first-year families and six balanced-window families. Official statutory filings extend extractive coverage, while original annual reports identify first reporting under the 2010 UK governance-code revision. First-year log-spread contrasts are -0.088 under equal family weights and +0.184 under market-capitalisation weights. Audit reporting receives 84.7% of the latter weight. The exact decomposition shows how policy composition changes the aggregate. Earlier voluntary practices and complementary liquidity outcomes limit a uniform information-benefit interpretation. No diffusion coefficient survives broad multiplicity adjustment. The evidence identifies the importance of policy mix, issuer scope and information timing in comparing reporting mandates.

1. Introduction
Reporting regulation can improve liquidity by supplying credible information and reducing adverse selection. The information increment depends on the covered firms, the audience receiving the report and the disclosure that was already available. We examine liquidity around reporting mandates and ask whether prior foreign implementation helps explain policy diffusion. Aggregation poses a further question: whether an estimate represents an average regulation family or the regulatory exposure of large issuers.

Following the empirical logic of Krueger et al. (2023), we establish the reporting obligation and public-information date before connecting them to issuer outcomes. The setting extends beyond comprehensive sustainability reporting to 17 economic regulation families. We then apply the competition, learning, emulation and coercion framework in Koning, Mertens and Roosenboom (2018). Geographic exposure and source-supported issuer comparisons are reported separately.

Across eight supported first-year families, log-spread contrasts are -0.088 with equal family weights and +0.184 with market-capitalisation weights. The latter assigns 84.7% to audit reporting. We decompose the difference exactly and report every single-family exclusion. The source evidence also distinguishes first required reporting under a revised governance code from first-ever governance disclosure. These comparisons establish the population and policy mix to which each estimate applies; they do not establish a common causal reporting benefit.

2. Institutional setting and research design
A public report and confidential CbCR submitted to tax authorities are different information events. Comprehensive mandatory E/S/G requires a response on environmental, social and governance topics, with the last topic determining the joint date. Climate-only rules, CSR spending and voluntary guidance are classified separately. The inherited topic review retains 37 qualifying phases from 41 country reviews; earliest topic histories remain incomplete. Bank public CbCR attaches to institutions and consolidation perimeters, not automatically every listed holding company.

Eligibility and timing are rule specific (Table 1). Companies House identifies Rio Tinto plc’s FY 2015 statutory extractive filing on 29 June 2016. Each of its four downloadable CSVs confirms that date. The voluntary Taxes paid release on 28 June remains a separate event. Equivalent official filings identify Anglo American on 30 June and Ferrexpo on 28 June. Fresnillo’s full statutory report is public in an issuer announcement on 15 June; its registry filing day remains unknown. These UK parents are connected to historical ordinary shares and original all-data reports; Rio’s Australian sibling is not counted as another treated issuer.

The governance study examines the 2010 UK Code revision, applicable to financial years beginning on or after 29 June 2010. BAE, Pearson, GKN, Drax and ITV explicitly report against it for FY 2011, public in 2012. Explained departures at Pearson and GKN remain covered responses. Earlier Combined Code reports and early adoption are retained: Drax had already adopted annual director re-election. BAE’s dated publication announcement is 26 March 2012; ITV’s full report appears in its 29 March announcement. Other cases retain year precision where approval, mailing or submission dates do not establish the first public day. This is a revised-code reporting cycle, not initial UK governance adoption.

The 2000-2025 panel contains 99,100 vendor quote/company identifiers. Outcomes are log median relative quoted spread, log median Amihud price impact and the fraction of valid observed returns equal to zero. Each requires 120 valid observations; logged proxies use annual 1/99 winsorisation. Calendar-year measures and observed-return denominators differ from the reference study. Exact bounded historical share identifiers corroborate the selected source matches; current names alone do not establish historical legal identity.

Within each family and cohort, we subtract the weighted comparison change from the implementer change relative to g -1. Geographic designs require known later implementation, 30 issuers per country and three comparison markets. Source-supported designs require three treated and 20 comparison issuers. First-year contrasts use g -1 and g; balanced support is fixed from g -3 through g +2 and averages years 0-2. Unknown histories are not coded never-treated.

GDP in g -1 is allocated over each fixed source-study country issuer universe, and cohorts are weighted by treated mass. Across families, equal weights assign 1/F and market-cap weights use pre-event treated capitalisation. Both use the same supported family set for all three outcomes; banking enters once. Equal-issuer and issuer-cap within-family alternatives are disclosed. Repeated issuer-policy exposures are not a unique-issuer investment portfolio.

US comparisons use contemporaneous parent-incorporation evidence. Governance and extractive studies additionally require US listing evidence and withhold direct foreign-regime flags; IFRS requires auditor US GAAP language. Governance candidates are the 120 largest available nonfinancial parents by pre-event capitalisation. All source and outcome failures remain visible; subsidiary exposure, US governance changes and industry differences remain confounds. Country-sampling diagnostics accompany geographic exposure; covered-study intervals describe issuer sampling with shared-issuer covariance, not causal policy uncertainty. Sparse common legal waves and pretrends constrain identification.

3. Liquidity, report timing and policy composition
Table 2 Panel A reports geographic exposure. Fifteen first-year families yield equal-family spread -0.049 and price impact -0.072, compared with +0.015 and +0.009 under market-cap weights. Confidential tax CbCR receives 33.1% of that exposure weight. Nine families support the balanced window. Pre-policy differences and incomplete eligibility prevent interpreting these broad contrasts as effects on every legally covered issuer.

Panel B uses eight source-supported first-year families. Equal-family spread is -0.088; market-cap spread is +0.184. Corresponding price-impact estimates are +0.013 and +0.188. Figure 1 shows all families and their weights. The market-cap effective family count, 1/sum(w squared), is 1.39. The cap-minus-equal spread difference is +0.272 log points. The decomposition multiplies each fixed family estimate by its weight change. Every single-family exclusion is disclosed; none determines the main specification.

Table 3 identifies the studies. First-year governance support comprises five treated issuers and 40 comparisons. Its spread contrast is -0.155, price impact -0.035 and zero-return fraction +0.00026. Balanced support retains 37 comparisons. Mandatory response to a revision does not establish new information or a new governance practice. The euro-area crisis, UK-US industry differences and contemporaneous US reforms also affect this comparison.

The extractive study includes seven treated issuers and 35 first-year comparisons. The spread contrast is -0.008; price impact is +0.128. Balanced support retains 24 comparisons and spread -0.089. The positive statutory filings resolve parent coverage and dates, but not foreign-subsidiary exposure or the incremental information in earlier voluntary reports. Commodity prices, Brexit and company-specific changes remain competing explanations.

Six families support balanced years 0-2: banks, conflict minerals, IFRS, German nonfinancial, extractive and revised-code governance reports. Spreads are -0.085 and -0.084 under equal and market-cap family weights, while price-impact contrasts are +0.073 and +0.160. Figure 2 reports fixed-sample profiles and pre-policy differences. Changing support prevents joining the eight-family first-year contrast to this six-family path as a single dynamic effect.

4. International diffusion and conclusion
Following Koning et al., competition uses prior implementation among export-pattern peers; learning uses prior outcomes among geographic neighbours; emulation uses language, legal-origin and IGO peers; external dependence uses IMF credit and aid. Signals precede the focal decision. Family-year strata and country-clustered covariance accompany timing and status alternatives, with lagged income, growth and trade controls where specified. The ledger records documented phases rather than certified first-ever adoption.

The full matrix is refitted: 1,330 designs yield 433 stable focal coefficients, none surviving the broad Holm correction. Issuer statutory filings and a governance-code revision do not create new independent national first-adoption decisions. Later liquidity estimates are not inserted into earlier learning signals. Reporting mandates differ in covered economic mass and information increments. Policy composition therefore provides an organising explanation for their aggregate contrasts, while source selection, common legal waves and pretrends limit causal and diffusion conclusions.
